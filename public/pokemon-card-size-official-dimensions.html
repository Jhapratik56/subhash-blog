Pokemon Card Size: Official Dimensions, Sleeves, and Jumbo Cards

Updated July 25, 2026 · 7 minute read

A standard Pokemon card measures 2.5 inches wide by 3.5 inches tall. That single fact answers most of what people search for, but the details around it, weight, thickness, sleeve fit, and how jumbo promo cards compare, are where collectors and new players usually get tripped up.

Whether you are buying your first pack, shopping for sleeves and a binder, or trying to figure out why a promo card will not fit in a standard toploader, this guide covers every measurement that actually matters, in plain terms.

Standard Pokemon card size

Every standard Pokemon TCG card, commons, rares, holos, and ultra rares alike, measures 2.5 inches by 3.5 inches. In metric terms that comes out to roughly 63.5 mm by 88.9 mm, sometimes rounded down to 63 by 88 mm in casual conversation. This size has held steady since the trading card game first launched in 1996, so a card pulled from a pack today fits the exact same binder page and sleeve as a card from the very first sets.

Quick answer: 2.5 inches wide by 3.5 inches tall. That is 63.5 mm by 88.9 mm, or 6.35 cm by 8.89 cm if you prefer centimeters.

Thickness and weight

Beyond width and height, two numbers matter for anyone stacking, shuffling, or shipping cards.

MeasurementValue
Width2.5 in / 63.5 mm
Height3.5 in / 88.9 mm
ThicknessApproximately 0.012 in / 0.3 mm, often labeled 35pt in collector shorthand
WeightRoughly 1.8 grams per card
Corner radiusAbout 0.125 in, giving the card its rounded corner shape

The 35pt thickness label comes up a lot when shopping for card savers or rigid holders, since some products are built for thicker 55pt or 75pt cards instead. A standard Pokemon card is always 35pt, so any holder advertised for that thickness will fit correctly.

How it compares to other trading cards

Part of why Pokemon cards are so easy to store is that the size lines up with several other collectibles people already own.

  • Standard playing cards (poker size): identical at 2.5 by 3.5 inches
  • Magic the Gathering: identical at 2.5 by 3.5 inches
  • US sports cards (baseball, basketball, football): identical at 2.5 by 3.5 inches
  • Yu Gi Oh: slightly smaller, around 2.3 by 3.3 inches

Because Pokemon shares its footprint with sports cards, playing cards, and Magic the Gathering, almost any storage product labeled for standard trading cards will work fine, which is a big part of why the accessory market for this size is so large.

Sleeve and toploader sizing

Getting sleeve sizing right matters more than people expect, a tight sleeve slows down shuffling and can bend corners over time, while a loose one leaves the card sliding around inside.

  • Standard sleeves: around 2.6 by 3.6 inches, or 66 by 91 mm, giving enough clearance for smooth insertion
  • Perfect fit inner sleeves: closer to 64 by 89 mm, meant to sit snugly against the card before an outer sleeve goes on top
  • Toploaders: typically sold as 3 by 4 inch, which comfortably houses a sleeved card for shipping or long term storage

If you are buying in bulk for a large collection, ordering a small test pack of sleeves before committing to a case is worth the extra few dollars, since fit can vary slightly between manufacturers even within the same labeled size.

Jumbo and oversized promo cards

Not every Pokemon card follows the standard size. Oversized promotional cards, often given out at events, included in special collection boxes, or featured in magazines like CoroCoro, can run considerably larger. Sizes vary by specific release, but jumbo promos commonly fall somewhere between about 5 by 7 inches on the smaller end and up to 8 by 11 inches for the largest display pieces. None of these oversized cards are legal for tournament play, they exist purely as collectibles, so do not expect to sleeve one alongside your standard deck.

Japanese cards versus US cards

Officially, Japanese and English language Pokemon cards share the identical 2.5 by 3.5 inch standard, which is convenient for anyone who collects across both markets since the same sleeves and binders work for both. Some collectors do report very minor variation on certain Japanese print runs, small enough, generally under a millimeter, that it almost never affects how a card fits inside a standard sleeve.

Pixel dimensions for digital design

If you are designing fan art, custom card templates, or preparing files for a print on demand service, physical inches will not tell you what you need for a screen or printer. At 300 DPI, the standard resolution for sharp print output, a Pokemon card comes out to 750 by 1050 pixels. Add a 0.125 inch bleed on each side for professional printing and the full canvas grows to roughly 825 by 1125 pixels. For web use at 72 DPI, the same card translates to about 180 by 252 pixels, though that resolution is too low for anything meant to be printed.

Frequently asked questions

What is the exact size of a Pokemon card?

A standard Pokemon card measures 2.5 inches wide by 3.5 inches tall, which converts to about 63.5 mm by 88.9 mm. This size has stayed the same since the trading card game launched in 1996.

Are Pokemon cards the same size as playing cards?

Yes. Pokemon cards match the standard poker card size of 2.5 by 3.5 inches, the same footprint used by most US sports cards and Magic the Gathering cards.

What sleeve size fits a Pokemon card?

Standard trading card sleeves sized around 2.6 by 3.6 inches, or roughly 66 by 91 mm, fit a Pokemon card with enough clearance for smooth insertion during shuffling and play.

How big are jumbo Pokemon cards?

Jumbo promotional Pokemon cards vary by release but commonly land between about 5 by 7 inches and 8 by 11 inches. They are not legal for tournament play and exist mainly as oversized collectibles.

Do Japanese Pokemon cards differ in size from US cards?

Officially, Japanese and English Pokemon cards share the same 2.5 by 3.5 inch dimensions. Some collectors report very slight variation on certain Japanese print runs, small enough that it rarely affects standard sleeve fit.
